Using the Street Sign Capture tool

This guide walks through actually using the tool once it's installed (see INSTALL_GUIDE.html if you haven't installed it yet). No technical background needed — this is just clicking buttons and filling in a short form.

What this tool does: as you browse street-level imagery (Cyclomedia Street Smart or Google Street View) and spot a damaged street name sign, it lets you draw a box around it, save that photo, and record a few details about the damage — all in a few clicks, without leaving your browser.

Before you capture anything

  1. Log into Cyclomedia Street Smart yourself, the normal way (this tool doesn't log you in — it just adds a button once you're already browsing).
  2. Navigate to wherever you want to look for damaged signs.
  3. On Cyclomedia Street Smart specifically: click the ⓘ info icon in Street Smart's own interface at least once. This tool reads your position from that info panel -- without opening it, sign matching won't work at all, even though the Capture button still appears. (Google Street View doesn't need this step -- position is available automatically there.) If you forget, a yellow banner will remind you.
  4. Watch for a small compass icon in the corner of the screen — if it's showing, the tool is active on this page and has a position to work with.

Capturing a sign, step by step

  1. Find a damaged sign in the street image and get it clearly in view.
  2. Click the floating "📷 Capture Sign" button.
  3. The screen dims slightly. Click and drag a box tightly around just the damaged sign (not the whole street scene).
  4. Release the mouse. A panel opens on the right side asking you to confirm a few details about what you captured.
  5. Fill in the panel (see field-by-field notes below), then click Save.
  6. Repeat for the next sign you find.

If you started dragging a box by mistake, press Esc or just click somewhere without dragging to cancel it.

The confirmation panel, field by field

  • On street / From street — usually auto-filled from nearby city sign records. Double check these against what's actually on the sign; edit the text boxes if they're wrong.
  • Which street name is printed on this sign? (required) — click the matching button, or type it yourself in the box below if neither button matches or you're not sure. This is what's physically printed on the sign you captured, which is not always the same as "On street"/"From street."
  • Which corner is this sign on? (required) — click the compass direction that matches (the tool takes its best guess and marks it "likely"). If none look right, you can either type a corner directly (like "NE") or type the compass heading you're facing in degrees, and it'll pick the closest corner for you. If you're unsure, click "Not sure."
  • Intersection type (required) — is this a normal 4-way crossing, a T-shaped intersection, an offset ("dog-leg") intersection, or something more unusual ("Complicated")? Pick "Not sure" if you can't tell.
  • Damage category (required) — click every category that applies (you can pick more than one, e.g. a sign can be both faded and vandalized). Click "No damage" if the sign is actually fine — this one cancels out any other damage selections, since a sign can't be both damaged and not damaged.
  • Notes (optional) — anything else worth mentioning in your own words.

Required fields are marked with a red *. If you try to save without filling them in, the tool will show a message and highlight what's missing — nothing is lost, just fill in the missing pieces and click Save again.

About the "outside your loaded SIMS data area" message

Sometimes you'll see a small warning saying the location is outside the loaded data area. This is not an error and doesn't mean anything is broken. It just means the tool doesn't have city sign records loaded for that specific spot, so it can't auto-fill the street names for you. You can still capture and save the sign normally — just fill in the street name yourself by typing what's printed on the sign. If you see this message in an area you'd expect to be covered, mention it to the project maintainer along with the location — they're actively tracking reports like that.

Checking your progress and getting your data out

Click the extension's icon in the Chrome toolbar (the pin icon from the install guide) to open a small popup:

  • Count — how many signs you've captured (persists across restarts, not just this session).
  • Export CSV — downloads a spreadsheet listing every sign you've captured, with all the details you entered.
  • Clear all — erases everything captured this session. There's no undo, so only use this if you mean to start over, or if the project maintainer asks you to.

Captured photos and the exported CSV save to your normal Downloads folder, like any other file downloaded in Chrome, with the date/time and street name in the filename.
